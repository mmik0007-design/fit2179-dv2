Part 2

Meet the Species We Could Lose

Behind every number is a living creature. Malaysia's most iconic wildlife are among those teetering on the edge.

🐯

Malayan Tiger

Panthera tigris jacksoni
Critically Endangered
Once roaming across the entire peninsula, the Malayan Tiger now clings to survival in fragmented forest patches.
~150 left in the wild
🦧

Bornean Orangutan

Pongo pygmaeus
Critically Endangered
Borneo's forests are the only home of this great ape. Palm oil expansion has destroyed more than half their habitat.
~104,700 left — down 50% since 1999
🦏

Sumatran Rhinoceros

Dicerorhinus sumatrensis
Critically Endangered
Malaysia's Sumatran Rhino is functionally extinct — the last individuals died in captivity in 2019.
Extinct in Malaysia since 2019
🐘

Bornean Pygmy Elephant

Elephas maximus borneensis
Endangered
The world's smallest elephants roam Sabah's forests. As logging fragments their habitat, herds face increasing human conflict.
~1,500 remaining
🐆

Malayan Tapir

Tapirus indicus
Endangered
One of the world's oldest mammals, the tapir needs large connected forest areas to survive.
Population declining rapidly
🦅

Wallace's Hawk-Eagle

Nisaetus nanus
Endangered
This forest-dependent raptor requires large tracts of mature lowland forest to survive.
Fewer than 2,500 mature individuals
“Malaysia ranks as the 21st most biodiverse country in the world, with 2,199 endemic species. If Malaysia fails to conserve them, extinction will result.”
Part 3

The Forest is Disappearing

From 2001 to 2023, Malaysia lost over 10 million hectares of tree cover — roughly the size of Portugal. Palm oil expansion and logging are the primary drivers.

Where is Forest Cover Being Lost?

Forest loss is not uniform across Malaysia. Sabah and Sarawak in Borneo have experienced the most dramatic deforestation.
